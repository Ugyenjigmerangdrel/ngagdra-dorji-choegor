# Source integration notes

Primary source: *Establishment of Retreat Centers in Eastern Bhutan*, His Eminence Drub-La Gyeltshen Trulku Rinpoche.

## Tibetan and Dzongkha transcription TODOs

The PDF text layer contains damaged characters. The website therefore uses English terminology while verified Unicode transcription is requested for:

- Hey Vajra
- Chakrasamvara mandala
- Dampa Rigja
- Longevity Buddha mandala
- Gayjor Duenpa
- Guru Dewa and the title of its supplementary practice manual
- The Yogic Daken title
- The Kalachakra name on the Tsholing Gongma page
- The Tibetan wording on the publication's final page

The single Tibetan letter described in the traditional account on page 4 is legible, but has not been reproduced in public copy because the full passage is reserved for a later long-form archival treatment.

## Ambiguities and contradictions for review

- Thruelzhig Gyeltshen Marpo is dated 1875–1950 on page 2 and 1874–1950 on page 5. The site follows the fuller biographical account on page 2 and flags the discrepancy here.
- Page 15 gives Lama Sangay Zangpo's life as 1727–1786, then says he returned to build a monastery in 1790. This chronology cannot be reconciled without another source, so the centre page avoids assigning a date to the original temple.
- Page 17 dates Larjab Drakar Choeling to 1701–1707 but describes its founder as a disciple of the 9th Je Khenpo, whose dates are printed as 1710–1759. These dates require historical review.
- Page 32 says Thruelzhig Gyeltshen Marpo taught at Mongar Dzong in the “early 90s.” Given his death in 1950, the intended decade is unclear.
- The publication credits Rinpoche with work on more than fifteen retreat centres, seven monasteries and a nunnery, while the project brief also mentions “more than eleven retreat centres supported.” Neither is presented as a current network count.
- The spelling of Tashi Yangtse and Trashiyangtse, Gongthung and Gonthung, and several names varies within the publication. The current site uses the spellings in the project brief pending an approved style list.
- The institute is described as founded in 2007, while an existing image caption identifies development views as 2006 and 2020. The 2006 image can document the site before formal foundation but its caption should be confirmed.

## Source material not yet used

- Most miraculous and visionary accounts are reserved for future long-form pages. The public site includes only brief, clearly attributed references where they explain a centre's origin.
- Detailed dimensions, superlatives and uniqueness claims for statues and mandalas are omitted pending measurement and editorial verification.
- Royal and government funding details for individual centres are not comprehensively reproduced.
- Detailed current practitioner counts and infrastructure appeals are retained in internal data or verification notes, not presented as current facts.
- The extended quotation by H.H. the 70th Je Khenpo on page 28 is not yet used.
- Most photographs embedded in centre pages of the PDF are not yet used as standalone web assets. Image extraction, rights, captions and meaningful alt text need review.
- The traditional account of the letter appearing among Thruelzhig Gyeltshen Marpo's remains is reserved for a dedicated archival treatment.
